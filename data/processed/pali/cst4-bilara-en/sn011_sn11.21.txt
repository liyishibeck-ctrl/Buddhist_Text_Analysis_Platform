Saṁyutta Nikāya 11.21 ||| Linked Discourses 11.21
3. Tatiyavagga ||| Chapter Three
Chetvāsutta ||| Slain
Sāvatthiyaṁ jetavane. ||| Near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove.
Atha kho sakko devānamindo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ aṭṭhāsi. ||| And then Sakka, lord of gods, went up to the Buddha, bowed, stood to one side,
Ekamantaṁ ṭhito kho sakko devānamindo bhagavantaṁ gāthāya ajjhabhāsi: ||| and addressed him in verse:
“Kiṁsu chetvā sukhaṁ seti, ||| “When what is slain do you sleep at ease?
kiṁsu chetvā na socati; ||| When what is slain is there no sorrow?
Kissassu ekadhammassa, ||| What is the one thing
vadhaṁ rocesi gotamā”ti. ||| whose killing you approve?”
“Kodhaṁ chetvā sukhaṁ seti, ||| “When anger’s slain you sleep at ease.
kodhaṁ chetvā na socati; ||| When anger’s slain there is no sorrow.
Kodhassa visamūlassa, ||| Anger has a poisonous root,
madhuraggassa vāsava; ||| and a sweet tip, O Vāsava.
Vadhaṁ ariyā pasaṁsanti, ||| The noble ones praise its killing,
tañhi chetvā na socatī”ti. ||| for when it’s slain there is no sorrow.”
